Sheet R-02The buyer

The person who opens the email usually can't sign the check

Property managers

Need an owner to decide

They manage someone else's building. Their job is getting a decision out of the owner, and they need something credible to forward.

HOA and condo boards

Fear the special assessment

A roof replacement penciled into the reserve study can turn into a special assessment. A restoration funded from reserves avoids that fight.

Owner-occupiers

Capital already committed

The replacement is in next year's capital plan. Deferring it frees that money for the business.

Asset managers

Timing inside a hold

A replacement is a capital event that lands in one year. On a defined hold, when it happens is the whole argument.

Both campaigns are built around that reality: give these people something credible to bring to whoever controls the money, and build the relationship before the ask.

Campaign 1Sheet R-05How it runs

From first email to closeout

STAGE 1

Cold email

Three emails of 30 to 50 words from Mike. One pain, one proof, one soft ask. No links.

STAGE 2

The reply

The AI sales rep answers in Mike's voice, qualifies the roof and offers three times from his calendar.

STAGE 3

The audit

Mike on the roof, about an hour, to the same protocol every time.

STAGE 4

The finding

Signed report plus the one-page summary, delivered together.

STAGE 5

Proposal

System, warranty term and cost against their replacement number.

STAGE 6

Closeout

As-built scope, photos and warranty for the client's file.

  • The reply books the audit itself, not a phone call. The audit is the meeting.
  • Qualified before booking: flat or low-slope, roof size and age, who approves capital work, and whether a replacement is already budgeted.
Mike or nobody

Every audit is Mike's. When his 10 to 12 weekly audit slots fill, sending slows down. The standard never drops to fill a calendar.

Campaign 1Sheet R-07Why it works

Campaign 2Sheet R-08The podcast

The guest is the prospect

A show for the people who own and manage flat-roof buildings across DFW. Each episode features one of them talking about running aging buildings. Nimble never pitches on air.

  • The invitation is the outreach. "We'd like to feature you" gets answered far more often than a cold pitch.
  • The prep call qualifies the building. Portfolio, roof age, leak history and capital plans come up naturally.
  • The audit offer comes after the relationship. About a week after the episode airs, Mike offers a free Roof Life Extension Audit as a thank-you.
  • Their network sees it. Guests share their episode with peers, who are the same buyers.
A neutral show, presented by Nimble

Guests say yes more readily when it isn't a vendor's sales channel. Working title options:

Top Floor: Conversations with DFW Building Owners
Capital Preservation: Protecting the Buildings That Pay the Bills
Above the Ceiling

Campaign 2Sheet R-10The guest journey

From invitation to audit

STEP 1

Invite

A short invitation to people on the target list who aren't in the email campaign.

STEP 2

Prep call

15 minutes on their buildings and the topics they want to cover.

STEP 3

Record

Remote, 45 minutes booked, edited down to about 25.

STEP 4

Publish

Full episode, 3 to 5 clips, a post tagging the guest, a page on the Nimble site.

STEP 5

Thank-you

About a week later, Mike offers a free Roof Life Extension Audit.

STEP 6

Roof Walk

Guests who book become candidates for a Roof Walk episode.

  • Distribution on YouTube, Spotify, Apple and LinkedIn, plus a monthly email to the prospect list.
  • Produced by Silk Road Media, so episodes and clips come out on a fixed schedule without adding work for Nimble's team.
  • Recommended hosting: Dallas hosts and keeps it conversational; Mike is the resident expert in the Ask Mike segment.
  • Every guest signs a release. HOA and condo guests may need board approval to name a property.

Sheet R-15Guardrails

Rules that protect Mike's name

  • Mike is described as a Haag-trained inspector. The credential belongs to Mike, never to the company, and is never attached to work he doesn't personally perform.
  • Every Roof Life Extension Audit is performed and signed by Mike. If his slots are full, sending slows down.
  • No guarantees or promises about a roof's future condition, in email, on air or in show notes.
  • Guests are never pitched on air. The audit offer comes after the episode, from Mike.
  • Written release from every guest, and written owner consent before any Roof Walk is filmed or published.
  • Tax language appears only in proposals for owner-occupiers and asset managers, never on the show.
