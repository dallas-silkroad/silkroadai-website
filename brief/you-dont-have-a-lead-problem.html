The Fully Booked Brief

You don't have a lead problem. You have a time problem.

By Dallas Wymes, Founder, Silk Road AI Solutions·August 4, 2026

I spent 32 years in supply chain and logistics before I ever built an AI system. From AEI, now DHL, through Mainfreight, I sat on the operations side of expedited, vetting, quoting, and track-and-trace. So when I walk onto a freight desk today, I don't look at it like a software vendor. I look at it like someone who has worked it. And I see the same thing almost every time.

The desk that's drowning in its own success

Picture a two-person expedited and white-glove agency. Strong reputation, decades in the niche, the kind of shop shippers call by name. Leads are not the problem. They have more than they can touch.

The problem is that both people own every shipment cradle to grave, which is wonderful for the customer and brutal on the clock. Quotes land in bunches with no way to work them in parallel, so it's feast or famine. A load request that comes in at 6 p.m. sits until morning, and in expedited, a load that sits until morning is a load that's already gone. New VIN checks and small-vehicle fraud pile more admin onto every single load, and it's the kind of grind that wears you down. The follow-ups that actually win business get dropped when the day gets away. By Friday, the owner told me, she's braindead.

None of that is a sales problem. It's a capacity problem wearing a sales problem's clothes.

Why speed is the whole game

In expedited freight, the first credible quote usually wins the load. Across the wider industry, the average business takes around 47 hours to respond to an inbound lead, and well over half never respond at all. On a high-touch desk that gap isn't laziness. It's a person who is physically running loads and can't be in two places at once. So the real question isn't how to find more freight. It's how to stop losing the freight you already have.

These are the problems we fix

We build a small team of AI teammates that work inside the stack you already run, whether that's McLeod, Highway, or HubSpot, with no rip-and-replace, in your voice, and with you approving anything before it leaves the office. We start with the one or two that hurt most and prove it before we ever add the next.

Instant lead and quote response, around the clock. Inbound load requests get captured and acknowledged the moment they arrive, day or night, with the details gathered and the quote prepped, so nothing goes cold after hours.

Carrier sourcing and vetting. Automatic VIN pulls and verification, cross-checks against Highway, Carrier411 and Freight Validate, fraud flags on authority and coverage, and clean records handed to your TMS with no re-keying.

Never-drop follow-up and nurture. Every open quote and past shipper stays on an automatic follow-up cadence written in your voice, so the touches that win business stop slipping through the cracks.

Proactive track and trace. Status drafted to shippers before they have to ask where their truck is, and an alert to you the moment a milestone doesn't happen.

Client onboarding and back-office intake. New-customer setup, credit and carrier packets, and document collection, all flowing into your system instead of eating your evening.

And when you're ready to grow, client acquisition. The same engine that fills a calendar can fill a freight book: net-new lead generation to refill the pipeline once the desk has the capacity to take it.

What actually changes

We don't promise, we baseline and measure. On a typical 60-day pilot the targets look like five to eight hours a week handed back to the team, carrier vetting done in under half the time, every open lead on an automatic follow-up cadence, and zero dropped hot-load milestones. If the numbers aren't there, you walk. That's the deal.

The point is not to replace anyone. It's to take the busywork off the desk so the people stay the trusted single point of contact and can carry more loads without adding headcount.

One quick win, proven, then you decide

If any of this reads like your desk, that's not a coincidence. It's the shape of nearly every high-touch logistics operation I've seen. We start with one nightmare, lift it off your plate on a short pilot with no lock-in, and let the numbers make the case.

I'd rather be doing this work than anything else. If you want to talk through where your week is leaking, reply here or send me a note. Two logistics people, thirty minutes, no pitch.
