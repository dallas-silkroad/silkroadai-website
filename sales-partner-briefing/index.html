03 Who buysThe target account

The profile

06 How pilots are pricedOrder of magnitude

A one-time build fee, then a monthly operating fee

Typical ranges

  • Build and setup: $2,500 to $6,500 one time, depending on the systems involved
  • Operating fee: $2,000 to $6,000 per month, depending on how many systems are live
  • A first pilot usually lands at the low end. Accounts grow as systems are added after the pilot proves out.
  • Third-party costs billed at cost (software seats, ad spend, telephony) are passed through and not commissionable.

Dallas prices every engagement to the actual bottleneck. Use these ranges to qualify, never to quote.

Worked example: a two-person expedited freight agency

Two bottlenecks in the pilot: carrier vetting and VIN checks, and follow-up on open leads. Sixty days. $2,100 to build, $1,100 per month to operate, cancel anytime after the pilot. Later phases quoted as their own small steps: $1,200 to $1,500 to build, $600 to $700 per month each.

Success targets set on day one: five to eight hours a week handed back, carrier vetting time cut by more than half, every open lead on an automatic cadence.

09 DiscoveryThree questions that open the door

Ask three questions. The answer to the third is the pilot.

1. "Walk me through what happens when a new lead or call comes in. Who touches it, and when?" 2. "Where do you lose the most jobs or drop the most balls: after hours, in the busy season, on follow-up?" 3. "If you could hand off one thing tomorrow, what would it be?"

Then stop talking. Write down their words. Those words go into the bottleneck map, and they come back to the owner in the proposal.

You have found a pilot when you hear

"We get leads but nobody follows up fast enough."Lead follow-up and AI SDR
"The phone rings all day and we miss half of it."Voice AI
"We type the same information into three systems."Workflow automation
"I'm the bottleneck. Everything runs through me."Assessment, then a pilot
10 ObjectionsThe honest answers

What you will hear, and what to say back

"AI will kill our personal touch."

The AI takes the busywork so the owner stays the trusted point of contact. During the pilot nothing goes out without their approval, and it all goes out in their voice.

"We're too small for this."

Our pilots are built for two-person shops. The CPA firm and the freight agency we use as examples were both small. Small is where the bottleneck is most personal.

"We tried a lead-gen company."

So did most of our clients. This is operations work, done by an operator, measured against a baseline you agree on day one.

"Our software doesn't integrate with anything."

Neither did the CPA firm's. We build around closed systems by producing outputs that feed them. That is normal for us.

"What about our customer data?"

Data stays in the client's own accounts, is processed only as instructed, is never used to train general AI models, and a BAA is signed where health information is involved.

"What does it cost?"

"Dallas scopes every pilot to the actual bottleneck, so I'd be guessing. A recent freight pilot ran about $3,200 for sixty days with no long-term commitment, to give you an order of magnitude."
